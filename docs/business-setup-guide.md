# Clear Signs: business setup guide

**Information only. This is NOT legal, tax or financial advice.** It points you to the official pages. Check them yourself (or ask an accountant) before acting, because rules and figures change.
Every rule or figure below was checked against the official source on **2026-09-29**. Where a figure could not be confirmed on the official page, it is left out and the link is given instead.
Only you can do these steps. Nothing here has been done yet.

## Do first: HMRC registration (URGENT question)
**What:** Register as self-employed with HMRC for Self Assessment. Free.
**Rule:** The gov.uk page says the deadline to register for Self Assessment is **5 October 2026** if you need to send a tax return for the tax year ending 5 April 2026, and that you could get a penalty if you tell HMRC after that. It applies to people who have not sent a tax return before, among others.
**What it means for you:** The answer depends on **when you first took money for this work**. If that was on or before 5 April 2026, register by 5 October 2026. If it was after 5 April 2026, the deadline for that tax year is later (the same page and the "check if you need to send a tax return" tool will tell you). **[OWNER: tell us the date you first took money, see QUESTIONS.md question 1.]**
Source: <https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment/self-employed> (checked on 2026-09-29)

**Trading allowance:** gov.uk says the trading allowance is up to £1,000 a year of tax-free trading income; if you claim it you cannot also deduct other expenses; it does not apply to partnership income. It can change whether you need to tell HMRC (the page says that if gross trading income is £1,000 or less you generally do not need to notify HMRC). Work out which is better for you, or ask an accountant.
Source: <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income> (checked on 2026-09-29)

## Trading name and what must be shown
**Rule:** You can trade under your own name or a business name (no need to register it). If you use a business name, gov.uk says you must include your name and business name (if you have one) on official paperwork such as invoices and letters. Names cannot include "limited", "Ltd", "LLP", "plc" or offensive words, and a name too similar to someone else's trade mark can be challenged and you may have to change it.
**What it means:** Your invoices, quotes and terms should show your own name as well as "Clear Signs". Whether your address must also appear is not stated clearly on the pages checked, so read the sources or ask. [OWNER: decide what address to use if you work from home, see QUESTIONS.md question 3.]
Sources: <https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader/choose-your-business-name> (checked on 2026-09-29); invoice content: <https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include> (page found in search, contents NOT read in detail, read before using invoices).

## Trade mark search (before spending on a domain, logo or printed cards)
**What:** Search the UK Intellectual Property Office (IPO) register by keyword or phrase for marks like "Clear Signs" and see who owns them. Also check Companies House and Google. The gov.uk page does not state a cost for searching.
**Why:** A business can be made to change a name that is too close to a registered trade mark. This is a search only; a similar mark existing does not automatically mean you cannot use the name, so if you find something similar, get advice.
Source: <https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark> (checked on 2026-09-29)

## ICO data protection fee (self-assessment)
**Rule:** The ICO says organisations, including sole traders, that use personal information need to pay a data protection fee unless exempt. The ICO has a free self-assessment (about 10 minutes) that tells you if you must pay and how much. The Tier 1 fee is **£52** a year (£5 less by direct debit) for organisations with a maximum turnover of £632,000 or no more than 10 staff.
**What to do:** Run the self-assessment and keep a screenshot of the result. **[OWNER: do this and record the result.]**
Sources: <https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/> and <https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/> (checked on 2026-09-29). Self-assessment tool: <https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/self-assessment/>

## VAT
**Rule:** You must register for VAT if your total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over **£90,000** (the VAT threshold), or you expect to go over it in the next 30 days. You can register earlier if you choose.
**What it means:** Most new sole traders are below this. Write "not VAT registered" on quotes and invoices only while that is true. Watch turnover monthly once you are growing.
Source: <https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register> (checked on 2026-09-29)

## Insurance
**Rules (gov.uk business support):** The only insurance legally required is employers' liability insurance (if you employ people) and motor insurance (if you use vehicles for business). Public liability and professional indemnity are not legally required for every business, but public liability covers claims that a member of the public was injured or property damaged because of your work, and professional indemnity covers claims that your advice, work or services caused a client a financial loss. You may need them if you visit clients or a contract asks for them.
**What to do:** Get quotes for public liability and professional indemnity. **Tell the insurer you use a partner for printing and fitting** and ask whether their cover includes work done by the partner, and check the partner's own insurance too (see `docs/process/partner-job-checklist.md`). Buying insurance costs money, so ask the owner first. Amounts of cover: ask the insurer, not covered by this guide.
Source: <https://www.business.gov.uk/support/regulations-and-licensing/insuring-your-business/> (checked on 2026-09-29)

## Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax
**Rule:** gov.uk says MTD for Income Tax is required from 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income (self-employment and property income before expenses, based on the tax return for the previous year) is over **£50,000**, and from 6 April 2027 for those over **£30,000**. Search results also show a later reduction to £20,000 from April 2028 (that point was NOT read on the page itself). To use it you must be registered for Self Assessment.
**What it means:** Probably not this year, but keep digital records of income and expenses from the start (see `docs/templates/`). It may apply in later years as income grows.
Sources: <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax> and <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sign-up-your-business-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax> (checked on 2026-09-29; the £50,000 date was read on the second page, the £30,000 date came from search results of the first)
